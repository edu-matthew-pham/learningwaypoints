Getting started · Walk the loop

Three parts,
one sequence.

Unpack the curriculum, plan units, and teach from a curated library of lessons, or make your own. ACARA sets the expectations for what Australian students should be taught. Learning Waypoints maps the incremental steps to get there, in the order they're best learned. Every tool below works on its own, so start wherever the problem in front of you is. This page walks the three parts in order, with one thing to try in each.

2 · Plan

One unit for all your classes

The deep end. There are two ways to plan here, and both start from the sequence you saw in Curriculum. The Planner builds the whole unit for you. The Kit does the planning in the AI you already use.

⬡Planner

Pick a standard, check the sequence, and build the unit from it: lesson plans, the resources each lesson needs, a student glossary, slides and handouts ready for Prepare, and lesson cards you can paste into your LMS. Everything comes from the one sequence, so it lines up.

When classes move at different speeds, the Planner changes the width rather than the pace: a faster class does more with each idea instead of racing on to the next one. That's the idea the whole suite is built on.

Take a standard you looked at in ACARA Progressions and open the Planner with it.

Open Planner

◇Waypoints Kit

A set of plain files that carries the core of the framework to your AI, with the same waypoint data you saw in ACARA Progressions underneath. Paste one link, tell it what you teach, and ask for a lesson-by-lesson plan, a second look at a plan you already have, or where a misunderstanding or a thin foundation could cause trouble later. It reads the same waypoints as the Planner, so the two agree. It can help with resources and quick checks too.

Paste the Start here prompt into your AI and let it ask you what you teach.

3 · Lessons

Teach from the library, or make your own

What students and teachers actually use in the room. Tutorials is the library; Prepare and Audio are for making your own; Signal and Insight check understanding during and after.

▤Tutorials

Short interactive lessons in coding, maths, science, engineering, digital systems and AI, run in the browser. Students predict, try, and check as they go. Every lesson has notes for the teacher, written for anyone, not just specialists, plus slides and answers. Teachers can write their own or request one.

Open a subject, pick a lesson at the start of a trail, and work through it as a student would.

Open Tutorials

▦Prepare

Describe your lesson and Prepare writes a prompt that generates the deck. Paste the result back and edit it with the slides beside you. The same text lays out as a worksheet too, with lines to write on, blanks to fill, and notes that appear on your copy only. Present from the browser, or export to PowerPoint, PDF or Google Docs.

Load the sample to see how it feels, then try the Generate tab with a lesson of your own.

Open Prepare

♬Audio

Study audio that follows your unit: one episode per step, each in the mode that fits, an explanation, a worked example, two experts debating, recall practice. Made in your browser, exported as MP3s.

Best after you've seen a sequence in the Planner or ACARA Progressions. The episodes map to it directly.

Formative assessment

◉Signal

Students say how confident they feel at each step, before, during or after a lesson, and you see where every class is. No student accounts; they type in a class code.

Start a session and put the code on the board. Students join straight away.

Open Signal

◐Insight

After students work through a Topic Explorer session, export the summary and Insight sorts their thinking: what's on track, what's a misconception, what's new, and which misconceptions to pick up with the whole class tomorrow.

Run a Topic Explorer coaching session first, then bring its summary here.

One pattern, everywhere

The tool builds a prompt→ You paste it into your AI→ You paste the result back

Every tool that generates something works this way, and it's on purpose. Your content never touches our servers. There are no accounts, nothing is stored, and you use whichever AI your school already trusts. The tool's job is to write you a much better prompt than you would by hand: one that knows the curriculum, the structure, and the format it expects back.

Once you've done it in one tool, you've done it in all of them. The Kit works the same way, except the prompt comes from a link rather than a tool, which is why Ask your AI is the quickest place to start.

The idea behind it

What connects every tool is one idea: a waypoint, one step on the path through a topic, in the order the steps are best learned. The Planner and the Kit plan a unit around them. Prepare builds the slides and handouts for them. Tutorials teach them. Signal checks confidence on them. Insight sorts thinking against them. Audio narrates them. Because every tool speaks the same language, what you do in one carries into the others.
